# Craft rules

Generalised from real revision sessions. Each rule names the failure it prevents.

## Reading (before any draft)
- **Name the register from the text.** A mystical poem written in plain
  words is still mystical; "concrete everyday images" is not a universal
  virtue. Failure prevented: childish lines added to a Sufi poem.
- **Find "I" and "you".** List every reading (lover / God / self / everyone).
  Keep all live readings; do not pick one unless the user does.
- **Mood of the verbs.** If the whole song wishes ("-ayım", "let me"),
  one flat verdict ("-dır", "it is") sounds like a judge. Either keep it as
  a deliberate turn or recast it as a wish.

## Weave (test every edit against the whole song)
Before answering any edit, re-read the whole song and name:
1. **Spine** — the recurring root/image (e.g. göz/bakış/gör-). An edit that
   adds a line with no link to the spine is a loose thread.
2. **Direction** — e.g. outside → inside, low → high. An edit that moves a
   rung out of order breaks the climb.
3. **Signature** — the one unusual construction (a single inversion, a
   single command). Do not multiply it; it works because it is single.
4. **Pairs** — a stanza feels whole when its lines pair (sahibi/varisi;
   duyduğunda/gördüğünde). If a stanza feels loose, look for the missing pair.
5. **Shared verbs** — in lists like "X, Y olayım", two nouns share one verb.
   Changing one ending ("Y'den olayım") strands the other noun.

## Prosody and line shape
- Count syllables for every line. Mirror line shapes across parallel
  sections (11-10-6 / 12-11-6). Arches (short-long-long-short, e.g. 6-7-7-6)
  are easy to set.
- In Turkish lists, an ending can be carried once: "Aynanın pasıdır /
  Gördüğünün lekesi / Hakikatin örtüsü / Varlığın kiridir" — outer lines
  close with -dır, inner lines stay open.
- Long lines (12+ syllables) split at a natural joint, never cut words.
  Half-spoken verses carry long lines; sung choruses need short ones.
- Turkish stress, for every "listen-for" note and melody: regular stress is
  word-final and weak; exceptional stress is strong. The syllable before
  negative -mA, question mI or an ek-fiil after a noun or a tense
  (hasta-ydı, yapar-dı, yapar-sa → has-TAY-dı, ya-PAR-dı, ya-PAR-sa) is
  stressed; -Iyor stresses its own first vowel
  (kal-KI-yor, not kalkı-YOR). Full list: references/compose.md §3.
- Watch vowel collisions at word joins ("o olayım"), accidental names or
  words across a line break ("bir sen" → "Birsen"), and ugly sounds on
  held notes.
- One diminutive or one intensifier ("bile", "-cik") can tip a serious song
  into cute or chatty; test before adding.

## Hook
- Hook from the user's own words wherever possible; a hook-shaped cliché
  ("ne varsa ... o olayım") is worse than repeating their phrase.
- A changing chorus (same melody, one line changes) is strong when the
  change deepens the same wish (tear outside → veil inside).
- Count how often the key word occurs. If it saturates (e.g. 10× in 3
  minutes), cut it from echoes first, then vary one line.

## Self-review (before showing anything)
Line by line: wrong tense or suffix, wrong word, contradiction, unintended
second reading, repeated word within two lines, syllable outliers, lines
off the spine. List what you found and fixed. The user's own lines are not
exempt; keeping their words means keeping their ideas, not their slips.
